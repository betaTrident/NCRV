# Voyix Workspace logo exploration

Status: concept phase only. These files are not approved brand assets and must not be integrated into the runtime before the concept checkpoint is approved.

## Design brief

**Summary:** Voyix Workspace is a private personal shiftbook that helps rotational night-shift employees record and understand estimated night-differential hours.

**Audience:** Individual employees. The mark will first appear in the application rail, authentication surface, browser tab, mobile shortcut, and social avatar.

**Brand adjectives:** precise, steady, human, calm, transparent.

**Avoid feeling:** payroll-authoritative, corporate-HR, fintech, surveillance-oriented, trendy, or synthetic.

**Promise:** “Your night hours, accounted for.”

**Competitive landscape:** Productivity and monitoring identities commonly use blue or multicolor palettes, circular containers, radial constructions, monoline geometry, and generic geometric sans typography. Open territory is a broad, one-color silhouette based on a defined interval, boundary crossing, or visible record in the established Voyix purple family.

**Mark hypothesis:** A combination identity with a compact symbol and the exact lowercase `voyixworkspace` wordmark. The first round compares an abstract negative-space mark, a custom letterform route, and a pictorial-abstract continuity route.

**Must work on:** 16 px favicon, 24–64 px navigation and application contexts, app tile, circular avatar crop, paper surfaces, deep-purple rail, solid black, and reversed white.

**Constraints:** Preserve the exact wordmark spelling and lowercase treatment, Manrope-derived proportions, `#5F249F`, `#341A4B`, `#C3A3E0`, and the warm paper palette. Do not use semantic green, amber, or rose as brand colors. Avoid literal payroll, finance, employer, security, surveillance, and attendance imagery.

**Success criteria:** One explainable idea; distinctive silhouette; clean one-color reproduction; survives 16 px; immediately readable wordmark; no generic payroll/HR/time-clock connotation; compatible with the existing product without reading as a UI icon.

**Decision-maker:** The requesting user.

**Assumptions:** The explicit request authorizes the first official logo exploration. Existing “no invented logo” text remains a runtime safeguard until a concept is approved. The user prefers strategic variety over cosmetic variants.

## Research and differentiation

### Category conventions to avoid

- Blue or rainbow technology palettes
- Generic rounded-square containment
- Literal clocks, alarm clocks, moons, calendars, checkmarks, or dashboards
- Shield, lock, briefcase, employee silhouette, currency, calculator, or payroll imagery
- Thin monoline detail that collapses at favicon size
- Random V/W monograms, swooshes, sparkles, or “AI” stars
- Default geometric-sans wordmarks with no custom logic

### Word map

- **Night window:** aperture, gate, band, span, threshold, enclosure
- **Record:** trace, ledger, notch, tally, endpoints, retained interval
- **Midnight:** crossing, hinge, handoff, inversion, boundary
- **Transparency:** open gap, visible construction, exposed path
- **Personal:** single interval, quiet ownership, contained space
- **Calm/steady:** balance, broad weight, few parts, controlled rhythm

### Reference-library lessons

The library was used only to study construction and category conventions. The useful lessons were reduction to a few primitives, broad negative space, deliberate geometry, and symbol/lockup pairing. No library artwork is traced or adapted.

## Selected concepts

### A — Midnight Gate

Two broad, offset geometric boundaries form a controlled vertical interval through negative space. It expresses a defined night window and a cross-midnight handoff without depicting a clock. The construction must avoid reading as pause bars, brackets, or a doorway.

### B — Cross-Midnight X

A custom `x` is split into four broad interval arms around a deliberate central handoff. It provides a name-led route and makes the long lowercase wordmark more ownable. The construction must avoid generic crossing arrows or a close-button glyph.

### C — Quiet Arc

An asymmetric open arc and a measured terminal reinterpret the documented crescent/clock-tick territory without using a conventional two-circle crescent or clock face. It is the continuity option and carries the highest resemblance risk; it must be dropped if it reads as a loading indicator, letter C, or generic moon.
